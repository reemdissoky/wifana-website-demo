# Wifana — Three Website Directions

Static, Arabic RTL, mobile-first demos with real Lalezar Regular and Thmanyah Sans Regular/Bold fonts. Open `index.html` for the comparison hub; serve the repository root for deployment. All links are relative. No build or package dependencies.

## Routes
- `family/index.html`: Direction 01, Family Space. Multi-page benefit-led editorial site, broad household image, independent how-it-works / FAQ / registration pages. Recommended main site.
- `control/index.html`: Direction 02, Simple Control. Product-led, interactive written Telegram demo, navy/mint presentation, capability boundaries and setup pages.
- `start/index.html`: Direction 03, Start at Ease. Registration page as standalone ad landing page, brief intro, household image, complete form, FAQ and a discover-more route. Recommended paid campaign destination.
- Every direction includes `order.html`, a gated review prototype with disabled payment.

Social approval of Everyday Life applies to Instagram. These website directions differ in information architecture, section composition, navigation and conversion journey, not just palette.

## Scope and controls
No voice feature is mentioned. Written Telegram only. No live website modification, data transmission, browser storage, analytics SDK or payment calls. Form validation is client-side demo logic, not production validation. Forms accept phone, optional email, city/Other, interest category and consent. Success text explicitly marks demo completion.

Direction 02 has a reviewer-only conditional financing sketch reachable from its review bar. Tabby/Tamara names appear only in that labeled modal and in reviewer notes. They are not available payment methods. Do not add to customer pages until provider availability, fees, wording and terms are approved.

`config.js` is the central approval/configuration record. No date-based activation. `canActivateOrdering()` requires explicit enablement, every approval gate, and approved packages. The demo always disables payment. Production requires actual backend and payment implementation, not merely setting flags.

## Outstanding decisions
1. Visual direction and ad-landing approval.
2. Packages, prices, hardware contents/availability, free-month terms, delivery cost/timing.
3. Initial installation: live website says self-installation; earlier technical conversation said technician. Current pages explain initial physical setup without choosing either unconfirmed promise.
4. Supported router/device matrix, control limits and any study-site restrictions (not promoted here).
5. Privacy/data retention/analytics/security wording, required certificates, terms and refunds. Policy buttons are review placeholders.
6. Approved payment availability and cost, then production ordering implementation.
7. CMS integration, lead notifications/export/UTM and GA4/GTM access.
